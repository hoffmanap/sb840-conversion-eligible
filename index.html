El Paso Housing Data Project · SB 840 Conversions

SB 840 lets El Paso turn empty offices, shops and warehouses into homes

Texas SB 840 allows qualifying office, retail, and warehouse buildings within city limits to convert to multifamily or mixed-use residential — administratively, without a rezoning case. It's a tool the city can use toward its housing goal. Here's every parcel El Paso's own appraisal records say could qualify, built from EPCAD parcel, improvement, and property data.

The conversion pathway

What has to be true about a building

SB 840's conversion provision (Local Government Code §218.202–204) opens the door for a specific, narrow set of buildings — not a blanket rezoning.

  • 01

    Currently office, retail, or warehouse

    The building's on-record use has to be one of these three categories — restaurants, auto shops, hotels and similar commercial uses don't qualify.

  • 02

    Built 5+ years ago

    The structure must have been constructed at least five years before the proposed conversion date.

  • 03

    At least 65% converts to residential

    At least 65% of the building — and 65% of each occupiable floor — has to convert to multifamily or mixed-use residential; up to roughly 35% can stay nonresidential in a mixed-use project.

  • 04

    Permit filed on or after Sept. 1, 2025

    The conversion provision applies to buildings where a permit for the conversion was submitted on or after the bill's effective date.

What the city gains

A faster, cheaper path to more housing

For qualifying conversions, the process is designed to move — which helps El Paso add units toward its own housing goal without the city having to build or subsidize them directly.

Density

  • Up to 145 units per acre — El Paso's applicable density ceiling for mixed-use residential and multifamily development under the bill.
  • Height and setback limits for the conversion can't be stricter than what a commercial building on the same site would already face.

Process, parking, and fees

  • No additional parking required — a conversion only has to keep whatever parking already exists on site; the city can't require more.
  • Administrative approval once the standards are met — no council vote, no separate rezoning case.
  • No traffic impact analysis, and no traffic-mitigation payment tied to the conversion.
  • No new impact fee, unless the land was already subject to one before the permit was filed.

Bottom line

El Paso doesn't have to build this housing.
It just has to let it happen.

SB 840 hands El Paso a conversion pathway for buildings that are already sitting there — vacant or underused office space, aging retail, spare warehouse capacity — and lets qualifying projects move through administrative approval instead of a rezoning fight. That's a lever the city can use toward its own housing goal at essentially no direct cost, on top of whatever else it's already doing.

Methods & caveats

Built from El Paso Central Appraisal District parcel, improvement, and property crosswalk data, filtered to City of El Paso jurisdiction only, cross-referenced against Texas SB 840 (89th Legislature) and the EPCAD structure class code table.

Confirmed candidates have a specific office/retail/warehouse structure code on file. Potential candidates are commercially/industrially classed parcels with no specific code to rule them out, and need manual verification. Existing multifamily and other residential structures are explicitly excluded from both tiers.

This dataset does not evaluate zoning eligibility, the 65% residential-area conversion test at the individual-building level, or proximity exclusions (airports, military bases). See README.md for full methodology, assumptions, and a running list of corrections.
